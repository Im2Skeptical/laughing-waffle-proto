# N03e-3q pass 4 — what a 10 µs second requires

The exact-rule pipeline costs **18.665 µs per simulated second at opening and 22.068 late** on the i9-10900K. Reaching 10 requires another **8.665 / 12.068 µs** respectively. The largest common cost is writing the two observable Practice traces. Late Birth also spends appreciable time rebuilding and growing population tables. Changing the clock or coarsening its dispatch cannot close either gap.

## What the sampler found

Optimized MSVC, one simulation thread pinned to logical CPU 15. A second thread on CPU 12 took 1,600 asynchronous stacks inside the uninstrumented advance loop for each window, both before and after the changes. The 39-second seed-1 window contains seven Foods. The 11-second window from seed-2026-late/3200 contains one Food and two Births, including a year change. All stage times below are weighted per simulated second, including seconds when the stage does not run.

- **Opening Food, before.** Of 743 Food stacks, 717 contained Practice activation, 247 contained pending-event trace publication, 89 contained direct trace publication, 40 contained worker assignment and seven contained meal-stock consumption. The trace builder visits the civilization and site histories, copying provider receipts, traits and tags into their respective pages. The worker loop walks residency and faction bins and fills authored board slots. The measured staffing change reuses that result until a ledger write or board rebuild. Re-scanning local Edible is a small target here.
- **Late Food, before.** Of 347 stacks, 294 contained Practice activation and 45 contained meal-stock consumption. A shortage can invoke shared routing, ordered provider visits and currency payment. Route eligibility can be indexed. Available quantities can change between two buyers in the *same* Food phase, even on an unchanged road network.
- **Late Birth, before.** Of 426 Birth stacks, 215 contained joint-table publication, including 46 in key merging, 51 in summary construction and 19 in site-index rebuilding. Among system leaf samples, 34 had an Elder-column grow/copy directly above the allocator or copy routine and 17 had a joint-key-column grow/copy. Thirty-five Birth stacks contained per-person RNG counting. On the second late Birth, seven of 14 sites had zero births, maturations and new elders, with no age advance. These sites can keep canonical tables and still run the rolls. First writes to imported data have to normalize key order and derived summaries. The accepted change preserves that first write.
- **Measurement boundary.** These are inclusive counts within a stage. The timing CLI resets from a value copy every 39 or 11 seconds. Dynamic vectors can regrow on the first write from that copy, even when an uninterrupted warm run allocates zero times. Optimize and measure both snapshot-resume and sustained advance before changing storage ownership. The samples identify functions and loops. They do not measure hardware cache misses.

## Floor of the current schedule

The driver already dispatches by lunar and seasonal deadlines. With the captured one-second phase duration, all **39/39** opening and **11/11** late seconds have a lunar boundary and a phase drain. Vassal already has a next-check deadline. A paused or terminal state performs zero advances. A long-phase scenario has a different workload mix.

A diagnostic executable linked the production driver and numeric clock to the same 18-entry dispatch table with empty non-clock stage functions. It retained the phase switch, seasonal checks and all 60 binary64 additions to each clock. After one warm-up trial, five trials of 4,096 window repetitions gave **0.130 / 0.131 µs/sim-s**. This is a counterfactual dispatch floor. The real clock, prelude, Vassal check and final flush together account for about **0.47 / 0.50 µs/sim-s** in the observed stage pass, before the due work. Stage probes themselves have overhead. Pipeline totals are measured separately.

Trace/enqueue takes first priority, followed by population publication, RNG, staffing/Stock/modifiers, other Birth cohort work, other drains including retries, and other activation. For example, a receipt appended during a recipe belongs only to the trace pool. The ranges are engineering estimates of removable fractions, not measured improvements. Using the middle of these ranges gives roughly **10.1 opening and 12.6 late**. A 10 µs late second needs nearly the optimistic combined case. For example, saving 6.1 in traces, 3.0 in recipes, 1.9 in population, 1.0 in drains and 0.1 in prelude gives 10.0. If trace storage saves only 4 on late, that same plan finishes near 12.1. Re-profile after each experiment because reducing page traffic can change the cost of adjacent loops. A higher-throughput compiler or a synthetic long-phase run does not establish the ordinary end-to-end budget.

## Forecast and Pixel 6

At 32 sim-seconds per game year, 1,000 years in one wall second allows **31.25 µs per published second**. Extrapolating these short windows to 32,000 advances gives **0.597 / 0.706 s of desktop pipeline time**, leaving **12.59 / 9.18 µs per second** for summary, anchors, publication and other overhead. Neither ordinary unmodified seed survives the full horizon. A representative long-lived run needs an action journal or a separately labelled fixture. The existing N03g longevity fixture uses a 64-second phase and a different threat threshold.

The architecture assumes a sustained Pixel 6 big core takes 2.5–3 times the i9 time. At this pass's medians that implies **46.7–56.0 µs opening** (17.9–21.4k sim-s/s) and **55.2–66.2 µs late** (15.1–18.1k sim-s/s), before forecast output. At a 10 µs desktop pipeline and the architecture's provisional **1.05 µs** desktop summary plus publication allowance, the estimate is **27.6–33.2 µs** on the phone, or **30.2–36.2k published sim-s/s**. At the slow end the pipeline must reach **9.37 µs** to keep that allowance and still deliver 32k/s. Thermals, memory bandwidth and UI contention can change the multiplier. These are projections without physical-device timing or Android hash evidence.

The next decision for the supervisor is whether to prototype shared trace storage first. Test it with two retained generations, a complete per-second summary, 64-second anchors and random restores. On a physical Pixel compare the hash stream with Windows, then measure sustained throughput and restore p95/p99 under UI load. Preserve the 4/8 ms restore targets.
