Agreed with Cam on 21 October 2026. Authority for gameplay design decisions.

Use these pillars to evaluate gameplay proposals and explain significant design
tradeoffs. They set direction, including systems still to develop. Consult the
[Civilization milestone](civilization-milestone.md) for implementation coverage
and scope, and [AI context](../ai/ai-context.md) for engine constraints.

1. Build powerful engines from interacting components. Shared triggers, conditions
   and effects let players discover combinations with explosive, compounding
   results. Distinct mechanics, costs and weaknesses support different playstyles
   and combinations.

2. Assemble builds through randomised opportunities under limited space, resources
   and time. Players can influence what they find and choose between immediate
   capability, future capacity and speculative investments.

3. Develop a civilisation whose population, economy and institutions visibly
   transform as its capabilities grow. Their interdependence creates changing
   opportunities and problems, with challenges that test different strengths and
   vulnerabilities.

4. Use projection to make delayed and indirect consequences playable. Players can
   inspect how an intervention changes their civilisation's trajectory, understand
   the causes, and make decisions across different timescales.

5. Craft a customisable legacy across successive civilisations. Players can pursue
   long-term combinations, redirect struggling runs toward useful inheritances,
   and increasingly secure gains that require substantial commitment.

The themes are growth, ambition, sacrifice and inheritance in a dark fantasy world
built upon earlier civilisations. Ruins, relics and surviving institutions give
the world accumulated history. The emotional tone can emerge through play, with
room for flourishing, spectacular power and collapse.

The non-goals are:

- Prolonging runs through administration after meaningful decisions have dried up.
- A universal build or compulsory development path that every civilisation
  converges toward.
- Simulation detail or historical completeness pursued without a clear
  contribution to play.
