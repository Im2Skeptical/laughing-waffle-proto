# Patrons and forbidden capital engines

Question: can a distinct cult patron make each decaying enemy class recognisable
by colour, silhouette and symbol, while its most developed institutions consume
the population that keeps the civilisation alive?

This is eight additional descriptive Gold capital candidates (one Practice and
one three-cell Structure per class), four original cult seals and twelve dedicated
illustrations. The original 130-card review, neutral starting set, previous palette
art and simulation definitions are unchanged. No population-consuming operation
or active enemy AI is implemented. All numbers below are review proposals.

## Agreed for this study

- Priest **reference yellow/pink** is accepted after the earlier ochre and pale
  trials. Merchant **green** remains primary; its accent is under review after
  the olive/ink G3 trial. Warrior red/teal and Scholar blue/gold continue.
- Push the higher institutions toward cosmic horror and develop identifiable
  patron iconography rather than relying on generic decay.
- Cam explicitly confirmed: players may keep running captured population-consuming
  institutions, retaining the power and facing the population cost.

## Proposed identities

| Enemy class | Proposed patron | Silhouette / seal | Corrupted ideal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warlord | Unbending Crown | Three spear-prongs bound by one closed band; tall, rigid symmetry | Absolute obedience makes people equipment |
| Merchant | Last Creditor | Low swollen vault-maw and three hanging weights | Accumulation makes the debtor into property |
| Priest | Hollow Sun | Empty ring with three falling rays; no pupil | Belonging requires surrender of the self |
| Philosopher | Unfinished Index | Three offset rectangular frames on a common stem | The perfect record replaces the person |

These names and gods are provisional. Their presence is expressed through cult
objects, impossible building anatomy and ritual, rather than new boss encounters.
Frontier pieces could carry understated marks, provinces institutionalise the
shapes, and capitals reveal the patron as architecture. This does not make the
remnant factions more active or replace the larger looming monster threat.

The current seal is an institution's cult affiliation, **not faction allegiance**.
It sits below the quality jewel and outside the Stock tray on the actual card.
Its shape also appears in major ritual objects and building silhouettes, so the
identity need not depend on a tiny icon or colour alone. A later allegiance marker
must remain separate; capturing territory does not silently change that marker.
Test monoclass opponents first; a secondary splash may use the primary enemy's
cult presentation while preserving per-instance allegiance.

## Proposed population sinks

| Practice | Cadence | Victim | Base return | Reserve / prerequisite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Iron Communion | Summer | 1 loyal local adult Warrior | 4 Arms Stock | 6 other loyal adult Warriors; 2 kept Arms Stock |
| Final Liquidation | Housing | 1 loyal local non-specialist adult | 6 Currency Stock | 4 other loyal adults, including 2 Scholars |
| Offering of Names | Faith | 1 loyal local non-specialist adult | 2 Bone Stock; convert up to 1 opposing adult | 3 kept loyal adult Priests; eligible opposing adult |
| Final Redaction | Death | 1 loyal local adult Scholar | 2 Record Stock; 8 Research | 3 other loyal adult Scholars; 2 kept Record Stock |

Loyal means allegiance to the installed piece's faction. A victim is an actual
population death, including the person's allegiance, specialism and contribution
to labour, defence or research. It is not a labour token or conversion payment.
No fallback to another class, age, region or allegiance. All reserves are kept;
the Merchant's two Scholars can be among the four other adults.

Each rite acts at most once on its named cadence, consumes at most one person,
and requires all preconditions plus room for the complete base Stock yield before
payment. No partial transaction. Ordinary Stock multipliers may increase Stock
yields; victims, reserves, conversions, Research and the supporting Structures'
flat triggered bonuses never multiply. Excess modified/bonus Stock is lost to
normal capacity limits. Output and Capacity remain independent additive pools.

The four three-cell institutions provide useful capacity or Arms multipliers,
plus tightly limited first-rite bonuses for the other three patrons. Their
support must not create free extra activations or extra sacrifices. Five Practice
slots and separate regional Structure capacity remain; the trial does not add
slots. Priest as a population specialty is still a proposed full-map system.

For this review, produced Stock and Research are regional rewards; conversions
benefit the piece's faction. Precise resource ownership, worker allegiance and
institutional adoption are unresolved. The cards are descriptive: none of these
rules run, and no live corpse resource or new Stock trait has been introduced.

## Capture, pacing and failure modes

Retaining the power and cost after capture is agreed. **Run / mothball / replace**
is a proposed interaction, not a settled or implemented mechanic. Mothballing
would stop both payments and returns; players need a visible way to stop a rite
before its next activation. The cult seal can remain when allegiance changes.

The intended loop is discover a functioning but corrupt capital engine, inspect
its next payment and downstream losses, then retain it briefly, support it through
growth, mothball it or spend space/time replacing it. Useful old institutions
remain prizes. The increasingly costly local reserve is meant to limit passive
exhaustion, not make sacrifices free or force every build toward the same engine.

Key prototype questions:

- Do rites cause a meaningful power-versus-future-capacity choice, or become the
  universally best use of surplus population? Ratios and phase frequency need a
  real in-game prototype after the content direction is selected.
- A consumed loyal person reduces influence; reserve loss can stall a rite,
  weaken defence or upset a control threshold. The projection must show these
  consequences together, including pending competing rites sharing the same pool.
- Killing an inherited faction's own followers may benefit the occupier's control
  share while yielding resources. Does that make conquest plus sacrifice too easy?
- A full Stock tray must not destroy a person for no base return. Growth, rites,
  conversions and ordinary deaths need an explicit future ordering and atomic
  eligibility check; this study chooses no engine event ordering.
- Strictly recurring population payments risk admin or unnoticed attrition. Rates,
  reserves and mothballing must be evaluated on the actual forecast and board.
- Class silhouettes should remain distinct at thumbnail scale. Oversized patron
  imagery must not turn Practices into portraits or occupy their quiet lower half.

## Pillars

- **1 — Engines:** useful components combine with patron supports; the person lost
  also removes future engine capacity. Strain: an efficient sacrifice engine can
  dominate all other builds or spiral through an uncapped trigger.
- **2 — Constrained opportunities:** inherited power competes with replacement
  time, five Practice slots, Structure footprint and finite population. Strain:
  repeated manual decisions without new information become administration.
- **3 — Visible civilisation:** cult marks grow into patron-shaped capitals;
  renewal can visibly alter institutions. Strain: cosmic horror everywhere would
  erase the contrast with neutral and frontier life.
- **4 — Projection:** the face shows a negative population input and inspection
  specifies victim, reserve, cadence, yield and beneficiary. A future simulation
  must project workforce, allegiance, control and defence consequences together.
- **5 — Legacy:** enduring institutions imply earlier civilisations. No persistent
  cult inheritance or legacy mechanic is added by this study.
